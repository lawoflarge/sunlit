Sunlit computes the position of the sun, the moon and the centre of the Milky Way
on the device, for any date and any place. There is no account and no sign in, so
there are no demo credentials to provide. Launching the app takes you straight to
the Sky view.

NO ACCOUNT, NO NETWORK NEEDED TO TEST
Every astronomical and solar number in this app is computed locally by SunlitCore.
No API is called at runtime for any of them. Put the phone in aeroplane mode and
every calculation, curve and table still works, including the offline city search
over 34,106 embedded entries. The only two things that use a network are
the Apple Maps tile layer in the Map view and MapKit place search, exactly as in
any map application. The rays, the day path and the shadow drawn on top of the map
are computed locally and appear with or without a connection.

THE FOUR VIEWS AND HOW TO REACH THEM
All four are tabs in the main navigation at the bottom of the screen, in this
order:
1. Sky. The home screen, shown on launch. The background gradient is drawn from
   the real solar altitude at the selected instant. Drag the time scrubber below
   the arc and the whole screen moves through the day.
2. AR. Second tab. Live camera with the sun and moon paths, hour marks, and the
   June solstice, December solstice and equinox reference curves. See the camera
   note below.
3. Map. Third tab. Sunrise and sunset direction rays from the pin, the moon rays,
   the day path over the terrain, and a shadow projection whose object height is
   adjustable. Tap anywhere to move the pin.
4. Data. Fourth tab. Civil, nautical and astronomical twilight, solar noon, day
   length, the moon phase calendar, upcoming eclipses with local circumstances,
   the annual altitude curve, and export.

The place and the date live in a header shared by all four views. Changing either
one changes all four.

CAMERA PERMISSION AND GRACEFUL DEGRADATION
The AR view is the only feature that uses the camera, and it asks only when that
tab is first opened. NSCameraUsageDescription is localised in all ten languages.
If permission is refused, or granted and later revoked in Settings, the AR view
does not fail and does not block: it drops the camera layer and draws the same sun
path, moon path, hour marks and the solstice and equinox reference curves over the
app's own sky gradient instead. Nothing else in the app needs the camera.

Location permission is requested so the current place can be used. A place can
also be chosen from the embedded offline city list or by dropping a pin in the Map
view, so the app is fully testable if location is refused.

THE FREE TIER
Today, at the current location, is free forever, in all four views. That includes
the sun position and compass, the AR sun path, sunrise and sunset, all three
twilights, golden hour and blue hour, solar noon, day length, shadow length, and
the UV index and irradiance figures for today. Nothing in that list is time

UV INDEX AND IRRADIANCE ARE CLEAR SKY MODELS
They are not measurements and the app never presents them as measurements. The UV
index is computed from the solar zenith angle, a latitude and season ozone
climatology embedded in the app, the site elevation and the earth to sun distance.
Irradiance is the Haurwitz clear sky model for global horizontal irradiance, with
the Erbs correlation splitting direct and diffuse. Every place these two numbers
appear carries a visible clear sky model caption, and the App Store description
says the same in every language. Cloud is not modelled and the app says so.

ACCURACY CLAIMS IN THE LISTING
Solar position uses the NREL Solar Position Algorithm, accurate to 0.0003 degrees.
Lunar position uses a truncated ELP-2000/82 series, accurate to about 10
arcseconds. Rise, set and twilight times come from a numerical solver rather than
an interpolation, so grazing events and high latitudes are handled, and polar day
and polar night are reported explicitly instead of a fabricated time. These are
the only accuracy claims made, and each is verified in the test suite against
published reference values.

PRIVACY

reporter, no advertising and no tracking SDK of any kind. The App Privacy answer
is Data Not Collected, which is accurate. Saved places and the measured horizon
profile are written to a file inside the app container and never leave the device.
